# Venue resolution

The venue resolver processes an exact imported venue row or an exact venue observation from a published recording or setlist proposal. It may propose an imported-row default or a performance-specific override. A default does not rewrite source labels, merge rooms into sites, move recordings, or change playback. A show override takes precedence over an imported default in normalized reads.

The entrypoints are `NormalizationVenueResolutionProcessor.ResolveImportedVenueAsync(attemptUuid, importedVenueUuid)` and `ResolveShowAsync(attemptUuid, reference)`. `VenueShowClaimReference` identifies the published attempt, input revision, venue JSON pointer and, for setlists, account index. The caller cannot choose the show, canonical target, policy, evidence, or freshness stamps. `RetryAsync(newAttemptUuid, previousAttemptUuid)` rereads the source; repeating an attempt UUID resumes its immutable saved phases. `GetAsync` returns a historical receipt, not a fresh observation.

## Preparation and ownership

Imported preparation captures the current row through `NormalizationImportedCatalogService`, checks the producer's original clocks through the existing binder, and decodes its retained bytes. An absent imported row has no invented input. A primary repeatable-read absence probe retains its imported-row generation; an empty Work publication acknowledges that absence and an arrival makes the owner pending again.

Recording and setlist preparation authenticates a compatible published proposal and re-decodes the exact original input. Its original saved binding supplies the performance UUID. Missing performance association remains unresolved independently of the venue observation. All original contributing source input, catalog, mapping, identity and owner clocks remain required; the resolver cannot attach current clocks to historical source wording. A source move or new proposal requires a new attempt.

Global candidate retrieval, current assignments, reviewed exclusions, omitted assignment targets and owner contribution membership share one primary repeatable-read snapshot. The snapshot includes complete candidate-universe and observational-owner stamps. Existing accepted venue assertions may cite historical raw evidence: their current authority is their accepted revision and owner stamp, rather than an assertion that the old raw resource is still current.

Owners remain `(venues, imported_venue, importedUuid)`, `(venues, show, showUuid)`, and `(venues, venue, canonicalUuid)` for identity creation. An authenticated source without a performance is enrolled as `(venues, observation, observationUuid)`. `VenueResolutionOwner.For` derives that UUID from the original extraction account's stable Work owner, not its publication/input UUID or account ordinal; it is not a show UUID or an accepted venue identity. Exact account coordinates remain on each immutable attempt. These observations consume the global venue epoch and original source/binding clocks, so association arrival or candidate changes make them pending. Contributor admission preserves the complete original peer membership and its coordination keys. Only the resolver's own keys are retired on a subsequent attempt; peer keys are not permanently copied into its contributor.

## Decision and publication

The saved preparation precedes policy evaluation. `VenueResolutionPolicyService.ReadStateAsync` observes the registry owner even when its active artifact is null. A fixed empty bootstrap Work publication establishes the registry owner once without activating an artifact. The subsequent primary snapshot retains its exact owner stamp with the nullable active policy; activation therefore invalidates inactive-policy acknowledgments. Evaluation records the entire bounded candidate pool, pair features, conflicting authorities, all source evidence and the original active policy observation. A pair result or search ranking cannot authorize publication. `NormalizedVenueService.PublishVenueResolutionAsync(workUuid, attemptUuid)` loads the processor's saved stage; callers cannot supply a winner or an authority flag.

A match publishes an owner-scoped assignment. Creating a canonical identity is a separate stage with a stable attempt-derived UUID, followed by a new candidate snapshot. That second snapshot may account for the processor's own identity write but must preserve the original source clocks. It rechecks the chosen target, alternatives and exclusions before assignment. Accepted writes and their stage receipt commit in the same Work transaction. A published Work receipt may preserve a reviewed assignment and retain a new suggestion as pending; the resolver reports that actual outcome, not an invented accepted result.

Review and unresolved decisions publish an empty `AcknowledgeDecision` Work stage before becoming terminal. The stage consumes the original source, candidate, owner and policy-registry observations and preserves peer membership; it has no identity or assignment authority. It changes no accepted venue rows or candidate epochs. Candidate, source, editorial or policy changes subsequently make the enrolled owner pending. Missing sources, incomplete bounded facts, unknown association and inactive policy have explicit outcomes. Preparation records `Ready`, `SourceUnbound` or `UnsupportedQuery` separately from its snapshot fingerprint. Unsupported retrieval wording is retained in full and settles unresolved; it is never clipped into a different claim. Cancellation or unexpected failure leaves durable saved phases resumable. Original admission conflicts or publication staleness produce a stale receipt without replacing an earlier accepted assignment. Terminal replay performs no domain writes.

## Physical identity and evaluation

Names and raw locality are retained claims. A city string is not a resolved geographic key, and city or unknown coordinates are not a venue pin. Imported row UUIDs and unqualified slugs do not identify a physical venue. Room parents, historical names, dates, addresses, relocation and multiple applicable location assertions cannot be guessed from a convenient name match. A physical relocation creates a new canonical venue; a conflated imported row needs individual performance overrides before any safe default.

This increment implements fixture-only matching and creation classifiers to verify the durable protocol. Literal name/locality agreement is explicitly a test classifier, not geographic resolution or a production rule. Fixture creation requires an explicit `[site] ` marker and a separately evaluated creation capability; it does not infer site or room from ordinary provider text. No production policy is registered or activated. The 34 development drafts are not deployment authority. Independent adjudication, separately grouped evaluation, dated room/relocation negatives and measured candidate recall remain required before introducing and activating real rules, small-model or Luna classifiers.

When that same recording/account is reprocessed under a genuinely new publication and acquires a performance, the bound context captures its earlier observation subscription. The new bound assignment or negative acknowledgment publishes first and enrolls the new source proof under the show owner. A final empty `SupersedeObservation` stage then clears only this resolver's obsolete observation contribution, preserving independent peers. Its authority is the immutable successful bound handoff receipt, not a claim that the old source became current. The old attempt is never relabeled or rewritten. Later source changes invalidate the show owner, without leaving a second obsolete watcher pending forever. An already retired or unrelated observation owner does not add another retirement stage.

`NormalizationWorkService.GetPendingSubjectsAsync` exposes the persisted pending watermark. `NormalizationVenueResolutionStore.GetForSubjectAsync` recovers its latest enrolled immutable attempt, including admission before ticket persistence; a caller can resume that attempt or request a new one. `GetRecoveryAttemptsAsync` also lists unfinished attempts and eligible leaf-stale receipts that may have failed before any Work owner existed. A retry child hides its parent; terminal review/unresolved/missing/applied receipts use their Work enrollment or remain explicit attention/history. Recovery pages use UUID cursors, accept 1–200 rows (default 100), and reject a page over 32 MiB before loading checkpoint bodies. Header locks precede the separate byte preflight so a concurrent stage write cannot bypass that limit. Each page is an independent read; a newly eligible earlier UUID appears on a later scan. This is discoverability, not permission to reuse stale source clocks: inspect the reason and obtain a compatible current source publication when required. Scheduling remains separate. A changed raw page or catalog association must first be reprocessed by the existing recording/setlist pipeline, then supplied as a new exact published claim. A stale historical claim cannot be made current by retrying or acknowledging it.

An unknown published attempt, unsupported extraction version, unreproducible account or malformed imported decoder shape cannot safely enroll an interpreted venue claim. Those paths retain their explicit stale/unsupported result and existing raw/extraction receipts; they do not invent missing source clocks. They require corrected source processing or an explicit new supported request. Ordinary unbound and query-bounded observations are enrolled, rather than falling into this unsupported-source case. A missing candidate owner or catalog freshness stamp is corrupt/historical data requiring repair: it returns `context_freshness_unavailable` without acknowledgment. Repair-only owner registration is not assumed to advance accepted venue epochs, and an absent owner cannot be replaced with a fabricated observation. Unknown geography and bounded facts still enroll negative decisions under their documented clocks.

Trusted bulk correction continues to use bounded per-item existing assignment, identity and metadata review commands. Each item has its own expected editorial/domain revisions and receipt. One conflict must not imply success for the rest; no general review UI or new batch authority is introduced.

## Bounds

A saved preparation, decision or stage is limited to 32 MiB. A stage set has at most three entries: optional identity creation, assignment or a negative acknowledgment, and optional final observation retirement. A missing-row acknowledgment is a single stage. Source authentication loads at most 100 original inputs, 32 MiB per input and 128 MiB total. Candidate name retrieval returns at most 200 name candidates; candidate facts retain explicit completeness issues when any fact bound omits alternatives. Assignment and reviewed-rejection history defaults to 1,000 rows, with a caller ceiling of 10,000 in the context readers. Complete owner coordination is capped at 10,000 bindings. Overflow fails the whole operation; it cannot authorize a truncated pool.

## Deployment and verification

Apply migration 50 `AddVenueResolution` before deploying these services. Rollback requires pausing venue resolver writers; retain policy decisions, attempts and stages rather than deleting evidence or receipts. Existing venue correction and normalized read contracts remain authoritative.

Verification is through public processor, Work and accepted-writer boundaries using real PostgreSQL and retained synthetic provider bytes. Required cases cover imported defaults and recording/setlist performance overrides, ambiguity and unknown geography, room/history conflicts, reviewed exclusions, source moves, new rivals, peer membership, policy revocation, staged creation, rollback and exact replay. All 59 venue cases pass in the combined 179-case focused run. They include a real blocked checkpoint writer to verify the recovery byte bound after concurrent stage publication. The solution builds; fresh correctness and readability reviews are complete. See the [overview](README.md#progress) for broader verification. These tests establish the publication protocol, not production matching accuracy.
